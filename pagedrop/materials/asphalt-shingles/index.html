Understanding Asphalt Shingles

Asphalt shingles were first commercially produced in the early 1900s, quickly replacing wooden shakes as the dominant roofing choice across American suburbs. Today they account for roughly 75 percent of all residential roofing installations in the United States, and San Ramon's development boom — which accelerated significantly after the 1990s when Bishop Ranch Business Park attracted major corporate tenants — reflects that national trend. The vast majority of single-family homes built in master-planned communities like Gale Ranch, Windemere, and Norris Canyon Estates were originally roofed with three-tab or dimensional asphalt shingles straight from the factory.

Modern asphalt shingles are composed of a fiberglass mat core saturated with asphalt and coated with ceramic-coated mineral granules. The granules serve two critical functions: they deflect ultraviolet radiation that would otherwise degrade the asphalt binder, and they add fire resistance. Most products sold today carry a Class A fire rating, which is especially important in California's Wildland-Urban Interface zones that border portions of San Ramon's eastern hills near Dougherty Valley and Bollinger Canyon Road.

There are three primary product categories: three-tab shingles, architectural (dimensional) shingles, and premium designer shingles that mimic slate or wood shake. Three-tab shingles offer a flat, uniform appearance and carry a lifespan of roughly 20–25 years. Architectural shingles feature a layered construction that creates shadow lines and texture, lasting 25–30 years under normal conditions. Premium lines such as GAF Camelot II or Owens Corning Duration Storm can last 30–50 years and are engineered to withstand wind speeds up to 130 mph — a meaningful specification given the Diablo wind events San Ramon experiences each fall.

For San Ramon's mix of Mediterranean Revival, Craftsman-inspired tract homes, and contemporary new construction, architectural shingles are the most popular choice because they complement varied architectural styles while delivering superior impact resistance compared to basic three-tab products.

The Diablo winds — a dry, offshore wind pattern — routinely push gusts of 40–70 mph through the passes and ridgelines bordering Dougherty Valley and Crow Canyon. Thermal Cycling from Tri-Valley Heat

San Ramon's summers routinely see temperatures exceeding 100°F on south- and west-facing roof surfaces, while winter overnight lows occasionally drop to 32°F — a swing of 70°F or more within a single year. This thermal cycling causes asphalt to expand and contract, gradually cracking granule adhesion and causing shingles installed in the 1990s and early 2000s to shed their protective granule coating prematurely. Homes in Twin Creeks and Bollinger Hills built between 1988 and 2002 are now 20–35 years old and are reaching or exceeding their rated service life, making asphalt shingle replacement an immediate priority for many households.

During the atmospheric river events of January 2023, the Tri-Valley received more than 7 inches of rainfall in a 10-day period — well above the annual average of roughly 17 inches. CJ's Roofing responded to dozens of emergency calls from San Ramon homeowners in Gale Ranch and Windemere whose older shingle roofs developed leaks at ridge lines, valleys, and around chimney flashings due to water intrusion. California Wildfire Ember Risk

The east-facing hillside neighborhoods of San Ramon — particularly those adjacent to the Tassajara Valley and Dougherty Valley — sit within or near the State Responsibility Area (SRA) mapped by CAL FIRE. Flying embers from wildland fires can travel more than a mile and land on rooftops. Asphalt shingles with a Class A fire rating, combined with compliant underlayment and sealed eave vents, form the first line of defense against ember ignition, making them not just a smart investment but increasingly a code requirement for San Ramon properties near the Wildland-Urban Interface.

Asphalt Shingle Methods and Approaches

Full Tear-Off and Replacement

The most thorough approach, recommended for roofs over 20 years old or with any sign of decking damage. All existing layers are removed, decking is inspected and repaired, and a complete new roofing system is installed. This is the standard for most homes in Twin Creeks, Canyon Lakes, and older Bollinger Hills neighborhoods.

Overlay (Re-Roof Over Existing Layer)

California building code permits one overlay layer on a residential roof. This option reduces labor cost and installation time but is only appropriate when the existing shingles and decking are in solid condition. CJ's Roofing advises this method selectively — it is not suitable for roofs showing decking damage or for homes in high-wind hillside zones where added weight and reduced air gap can create problems.

Partial Section Replacement

When storm damage is isolated — for example, a Diablo wind event that lifts shingles on a single west-facing slope — we can replace that section while blending the shingle profile to the existing roof. Color matching is carefully managed, particularly for HOA-regulated communities in Gale Ranch.

High-Wind Upgrade Installation

For properties above 700 feet elevation in Norris Canyon Estates or Dougherty Valley hillside zones, we use 6-nail fastening patterns (versus the standard 4-nail), sealed starter strips, and hip-and-ridge shingles rated to 130 mph to meet both manufacturer warranty requirements and Contra Costa County's stricter high-wind zone specifications.

Algae-Resistant Shingle Installation

For north-facing or heavily shaded slopes near San Ramon's tree-lined streets in Alamo Creek or Bollinger Canyon, we specify shingles with copper-coated granules (such as GAF Timberline HDZ AR or Owens Corning Duration) that inhibit algae growth and maintain curb appeal without annual chemical treatments.

Asphalt Shingle Maintenance Tips for San Ramon Homeowners

  • Inspect after every Diablo wind event: Following fall wind events that exceed 40 mph, do a visual inspection from the ground or hire CJ's Roofing for a post-storm assessment to catch lifted or missing shingles early.
  • Clean gutters twice yearly: San Ramon's valley oaks and sycamores drop significant debris in fall and spring. Clogged gutters cause water to back up under eaves and accelerate shingle deterioration at roof edges.
  • Trim overhanging branches: Branches extending over your roof create moss pathways and debris accumulation — particularly relevant in Bollinger Hills and Alamo Creek.
  • Check flashing annually: Inspect chimney, skylight, and pipe boot flashings before the November–March rainy season to catch early failures before atmospheric river events arrive.
  • Apply algae treatment on shaded slopes: A zinc sulfate solution applied annually to north-facing shingle surfaces inhibits algae and moss growth between replacements.
  • Maintain proper attic ventilation: San Ramon's 100°F+ summer heat causes attic temperatures to reach 150°F, which cooks asphalt shingles from beneath. Ensure ridge and soffit vents are unobstructed.
  • Schedule a professional inspection every 3–5 years: Even newer roofs benefit from periodic professional assessment to catch minor issues before they escalate. Schedule your inspection with CJ's Roofing.
